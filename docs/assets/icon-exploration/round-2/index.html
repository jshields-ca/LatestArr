LatestArr icon exploration, round 2

Round 1 was mostly different ways of drawing an L. This round tries silhouettes that mean something on their own, and a second colourway (white on deep rose) beside the current rose and ink. These are still sketches, for choosing a direction. Directions can be combined, too (for example, marquee lights on a ticket). Strongest: G (ticket) and I (stamp), with O (marquee) as the playful option.

At a glance: both colourways at 96, 32, and 16px

G · Ticket

An admission ticket: notched sides and a tear line. New releases are an event, and the newsletter is your ticket in. The silhouette is recognisable at any size, and nothing else in the media-server space uses it.

Assessment: Distinctive among media apps. The risk is that ticketing and help-desk tools ("support tickets") use tickets too; the tear line and slight tilt help keep it about movies.

H · Arrival

An L whose foot turns into an arrow: “the latest, arriving”. It's one continuous stroke, so it reads as a single shape.

Assessment: Bold and simple, but in practice it reads as a “turn right” road sign or the ↳ “reply” symbol, which makes it a UI icon rather than a brand.

I · Postage stamp

A stamp with perforated edges and an L cut out: delivered to your inbox, without the envelope. The scalloped edge is a shape people know instantly.

Assessment: A strong “it arrives by mail” cue that's more characterful than an envelope. The perforations soften at 16px but the stamp still reads as a square with an L.

O · Marquee L

An L spelled out in marquee lights, like a cinema's “now showing” sign. It combines the name, the media world, and a bit of showbiz fun.

Assessment: The most playful and the most “ours”. At 16px the lights merge into a solid L, which still works. Worth refining the dot count and spacing.

J · “New” seal

The starburst “NEW” sticker from shop shelves, with an L punched through it: what's new, stamped with the brand.

Assessment: Ruled out after seeing it small: at 32px and 16px the round seal with an L inside reads as a clock face. K · L + A set square

A drafting set square: its upright and base form the L, and its slope forms an A, for LatestArr. It's precise and a little crafted.

Assessment: The cleverest monogram, but the least obvious meaning: it reads as “design tool” or a warning triangle before it reads as newsletters.
